### 1. In-store "do I own this?" workflow (themes into your later story)
*Use in: any thread about cataloging, duplicate buys, or "how do you track your collection."*
> Honestly my biggest pain is the in-store moment — standing in front of a bin and not being sure if I already own a pressing. I've bounced between a Google Sheet and Discogs, and both let me down when I actually need them (no signal in some stores, or the app just won't load). What I've landed on: I keep a stripped-down list of just artist/title/pressing on my phone that loads offline, and treat Discogs as the "full" backup at home. Not perfect, but it's cut down my duplicate buys. Curious what others do here — spreadsheets feel clunky past a few hundred records.

### 2. Grading: VG vs VG+ (Goldmine standard)
*Use in: threads about condition, grading arguments, or buying used.*
> The VG vs VG+ line is where most disagreement lives. What helped me: VG+ allows light scuffs/hairlines that don't affect play; VG has visible wear and light surface noise. The trick is checking under daylight angled to catch hairlines, and actually playing the quiet parts — visual grading alone gets people burned. I always assume one grade lower than the seller unless they're very specific about play-testing.

### 3. Storage / sleeves tip
*Use in: "how do you store your collection" or cleaning threads.*
> Two cheap upgrades that preserved more of my collection than anything: anti-static inner sleeves (replace the factory paper ones — they scuff every time you pull the record) and outer poly sleeves for the jacket. I also keep a separate "needs cleaning" pile so clean records never share an inner with a dusty one. Sounds obvious but it took me years to actually do it consistently.

### 4. First-pressing identification
*Use in: threads about pressings, "is this an original," or specific-album deep dives (only if you actually know the record).*
> For a lot of major labels, the deadwax runout etchings and the label design tell you more than the sleeve — matrix numbers, mastering plant codes (like Sterling "ST" or Capitol pressing-plant marks). Discogs is decent for cross-referencing once you have the matrix, but the sleeve alone is unreliable because reissues reused cover art. If you're hunting originals, learn to read the runout.
